# azork plan

Turn an Azgaar Fantasy Map Generator world into a Zork-style text adventure that follows one
of the world's journeys. Built in chat sessions with Claude; this repository is the source of
truth for code, worlds, ledgers and content packs.

## Decisions (2026-10-03)

1. **Playable area: the journey corridor.** Only places near a journey's track are generated.
2. **Text: data templates plus content packs.** Each world has `Azgaar/<World>/content/`, written
   in sessions and tagged canon or draft. Content packs are per world.
3. **Goal: the journey.** Complete the journey's stages; the plan is the clock and the stakes.
   Treasure (Zork-style) and knowledge scores may be added later as subsystems.
4. **Terminal first.** Browser and Inform 7 exports later.
5. **Ledger export on exit: keep, discard or fork.**
   - keep: append the session's proposed entries (status draft) to `<World>_ledger.md`;
   - discard: write nothing to the ledger (the transcript is still saved);
   - fork: write `<World>_ledger.<branch>.md` (main ledger plus the session), and allow later
     play to run on that branch.
6. **The master ledger lives in the repo** (`Azgaar/<World>/<World>_ledger.md`).
7. **Clock: dawn-based, simplified.** Declination from day of year, hour angle from latitude,
   local solar time. Start day comes from `content/world.json` (default: spring equinox).
   Stages under 24 h a day depart at dawn and spread their travel hours over daylight, so a
   full day ends at dusk; 24-hour modes never stop.
8. **Plan versus play.** The journey file is the plan and is never rewritten. Play may diverge.
   Faster modes from `transports_allowed` are offered where they fit; difficulty scales with
   the share of plan time saved (light < 25 %, moderate < 50 %, hard < 75 %, severe).
   Example: Lania stage 9 by rowboat is 146 days; a sailing boat saves 81 days (hard), a
   sailing ship 127 days (severe).
9. **Strict initial setup, expansion on demand.** Before a story is generated, every required
   file must be present: stops (stage endpoints), canon places (ledger anchors inside the
   corridor) and sites on the track with a Watabou generator (dungeons). Everything else in
   the corridor is described from map data while passing; entering it pauses play with a
   request for its file (`setup.expansion_message`), then the area is generated.
10. **Dwellings are generated by the engine.** A Dwellings export is used when one has been
    assigned; it is never required.

## Decisions (2026-10-04)

11. **One Page Dungeon door types** (from the user): 0 connection and 2 archway are open;
    1 door blocks vision when closed; 3 stairs is an entrance; 4 portcullis can be raised;
    5 is locked and its key is somewhere in the dungeon; 6 is secret; 7 barred is impassable;
    8 long stairs is an exit; 9 steps behaves as a door with a change of level.
    Directions use +x east, +y south (checked against three dungeons' notes).
12. **Required places stay dynamic.** Stops come from the journey, canon places from ledger
    anchors, sites from markers; nothing is hard-coded per world.
13. **Fully itemized inventory with weights.** Light up to 15 kg, laden up to 35 kg (content
    pack). Dropping anything is allowed and always shows the load change.
14. **Dropping cargo has consequences tied to the story arc** (phase 5 design): items can carry
    hidden needs (a story beat that requires them). When the beat arrives without the item, the
    consequence was fixed when the story was generated and fits the arc: being stuck, failing
    the mission, or a fatal encounter. The player is never told for certain what will be needed;
    hints come through people, notes and EXAMINE.

## Decisions (2026-10-04, after play-testing)

15. **Type 9 steps** are an open passage with a change of level; types 3 and 8 enter and leave.
16. **Start at the journey's beginning** with a short generated hook (phase 5). The Party marker is
    one of Azgaar's default markers (one per map, at a random burg, note "Current location of the
    adventuring party") and is not tied to journeys; how to use it is an open question.
17. **Hints are diverse:** people (topics drawn from map data and content packs), letters and
    books found by searching, map fragments, sounds (LISTEN), and the player's own JOURNAL
    (WRITE <text>, JOURNAL), which needs a journal and something to write with.
18. **Zork-style death:** instant, with UNDO and RESTORE.
19. **Every scene is a container for purpose, weighed against the timetable** (design for
    phases 3 and 5): each place holds story elements, sub-stories, hooks and catalysts. Time
    spent inside is plan time; stopping must be worth it. The deepest part of a dungeon (or an
    important dwelling) holds what its story names and should repay the time invested.

## Decisions (2026-10-04, story)

20. **The Party marker is a meeting point:** if it lies near the route, a contact waits in that
    burg; meeting them is a beat of the main story.
21. **The player judges time.** SCHEDULE (or ON TRACK) says how far ahead or behind the plan the
    party is, when the plan expects the next stop, and how much slack the deadline leaves.
22. **Dwellings are placed by clicking:** `python -m azork pick <World> <burg> --dwelling <file>`
    opens the town plan in the browser; click the building, name the house, Save.
23. **Zork-style hunger and thirst:** death after three days without water or three weeks without food.

## Decisions (2026-10-04, phases 3 and 4)

24. **The contact and the follower stay ambiguous** on purpose.
25. **Side stories hook by chance, plus planted hooks:** each campaign plants an heirloom ring in the
    first lead dungeon and an unsent letter at the first town stop; both always start a side story.
26. **Missing a stop has a story cost:** each stop warns of the hazards before the next one
    (borders, zones). Miss the stop and luck decides at the hazard: you get through, or you lose
    12-96 hours.
27. **Ledger and story documents are split:** the ledger gets a short session entry with a link;
    the story details (every storyline, phase, element, the score and the clock) go to
    `<World>_story.md` beside it (or `<World>_story.<branch>.md` for a fork).
28. **Play the whole game or single scenes:** `python -m azork play <World>` runs the campaign on the
    road; `--scene` plays one place on its own (with the campaign if there is one).
29. **Missing files pause the game:** a town without a plan, or a story person who needs a house where
    none is placed, stops with three choices: PROVIDE the file (and click its building with
    `azork pick`), GENERATE a made-up one here, or ALWAYS generate from now on.

## Decisions (2026-10-05, direction, grid and streets)

30. **Direction:** a new game opens with the hook; a saved game opens with "Previously" and the goals.
    Main-story text is gold, side stories teal (ANSI; `--no-color` or NO_COLOR turns it off).
    New goals are announced with their in-world source; on arrival where a goal lies it becomes
    concrete (who, which building, which street). RECAP prints the story so far.
31. **The road is the map's hex grid** (Azgaar's pointy-hex overlay at the saved scale and offset: 5 mi
    on Lania). One hex per move. The planned route keeps the plan's pace; roads are quicker (0.75)
    and trails (0.9) but watched; the wild off the route is slower by the biome's cost in the map.
    ROUTE explains the choice; CAMP rests an hour by day or sleeps to dawn with a bedroll (a few
    fitful hours without); FORAGE and HUNT by biome odds; water from rivers and wetland.
32. **Towns are streets of real buildings:** roads cut into stretches, every building of the plan
    on its stretch, unknown until SEARCH, directions or the story reveal it. Key buildings (inn,
    hall, temple, storehouse, harbour office, keep) are real buildings chosen by fit. You enter on
    the side you arrive from. Interiors are built on entry from a placed Dwellings export
    (`azork pick --role inn` places one for a key building), or made up (the game asks).
33. **People:** looks from kin (the culture's namebase) and climate; bios (origin, tradition, faith)
    for keepers and carriers; anyone gives directions; named things in the map can be explained.
34. **Carriers** (carters at the way in, boatmen by the harbour office, travellers on roads) offer
    passage ahead: price by distance, double across faiths, refused across hostile borders without
    papers. A registered writ (the clerk stamps it) counts as papers.
35. **Parts of things:** anything mentioned in what you can see can be examined; small things
    (cups, books, notices...) can be taken one at a time.

## Story framework (phase 5, first version)

- **Storylines** (main, lead, side) are sequences of **phases**. A phase has an objective, triggers
  (start, at a place, pass a point on the journey, talk, ask about, give, take, have, examine,
  read, flag), requirements (flags from any storyline, any-of groups), points, a position on the
  journey, and an optional deadline. Completing a phase awards points, sets flags, can hand over
  items, can save time, and can end the game.
- **Elements** are what a storyline needs in the world (people, items, clues), anchored to a burg,
  a marker or a scene (optionally to the deepest room, or a preferred room purpose), or carried.
  Scenes spawn the elements anchored to them when they are built.
- **Passing on by:** a phase behind the party's position that is not required is marked missed
  when the party moves past it. Stop, or pass by: both are choices with costs.
- **The main arc** comes from the journey (azork/story/arc.py, genres.json): genre by journey type
  (19 Azgaar types), quarry and destination from the journey title, the commission token, beats at
  planned stays, passive beats for hostile borders and zones entered, the meeting point, the cargo
  before the first laden stage, and the climax with a deadline of the plan's length plus a tenth.
  The climax needs the cargo or a clue from a lead (two ways through). A content pack's
  `story.json` replaces texts with canon and adds topics to existing people.
- **Leads** are optional storylines for dungeons on the track: the rumour arrives on the road, the
  clue lies in the deepest room.
- **Side stories** are hooked by play (azork/story/side.py): a dying person's last request, a
  treasure's crest, a rumour about a site. Their elements are dispersed ahead on the route,
  preferring places that already have scenes; rewards can be money or time saved.
- **Campaigns** (azork/campaign.py, `python -m azork new <World>`) keep the story, the clock, the
  position and the inventory between scenes. Between scenes the party follows the plan's pace,
  so lost time stays lost; going back costs time. The game reports SCORE, GOALS and SCHEDULE.

## File conventions

- Town or village plan: `<burg>.json` (City or Village Generator export).
- Dwelling: `<burg>--<label>.json`, or placed by `content/manifest.json`, or by a ledger entry
  anchored to `building N in <town>.json` and naming the dwelling file.
- Dungeon: `dungeon-<marker id>.json`, or placed by `content/manifest.json`
  (One Page Dungeon exports carry no seed, so the file cannot be matched by content).

## Content pack (draft spec)

`Azgaar/<World>/content/world.json`:
`start_day_of_year`, `strict_mi` (default 2), `corridor_mi` (default 40), `transports_allowed`.

`Azgaar/<World>/content/manifest.json`: `files` → `{filename: {burg, building, marker, label, source}}`.

To come (phase 5): places, people, hooks and encounters, each with an anchor (as in the ledger)
and a status (canon or draft).

## Scenes (phase 2)

`python -m azork play <World> --scene dwelling:<file stem>` or `--scene dungeon:<marker>`.
Each scene has an outside room described from map data, the starting kit from
`content/items.json`, and an overlay from `content/places/<scene>.json`. The plan clock starts
where the journey passes nearest. A dungeon whose notes name no key gets one placed in a
dead-end chamber (seeded, provenance new).

## How interiors get purpose (phase 2b)

- Dwelling rooms keep Watabou's names (kitchen, library...); generic rooms get a purpose for
  their floor, seeded; overlays rename by Watabou room id (`house:<floor>:<room>`), which is stable.
- Dungeon chambers take purposes from a theme read from the title and story (palace, temple,
  tomb, default), their size, water and shape. The deepest chamber (locked doors count 3 steps,
  secret doors 2) is the heart: vault for a hidden treasure, lair for a creature.
- Notes become things with initial descriptions that disappear once the thing is moved
  ("A key lies at the bottom of a small pool"); supports and containers stay as scenery.
- Furnishings come from `azork/data/furnishings.json` by purpose, about one room in eight has
  a joke where something is missing; searchable furniture gives loot, better deeper down.
- People know topics built from map data (their town, ruler, faith, nearby burgs and sites,
  hearsay about the journey's stops), plus topics from content packs, which take priority.
- Dwellings: `python -m azork place <World> <file> --burg <name>` ranks buildings by outline;
  `--best` or `--building N` records the placement and keeps the file's own name as the label.
